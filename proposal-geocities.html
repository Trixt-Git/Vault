THE GARY PROBLEM

A tough request comes in. Nobody searches the folder; they ask Gary. Gary glances up: "We ran that exact spec for Hendricks in August—five thousand on 100-pound gloss. Call it eighteen-fifty." And he's right.

Gary finally gets to take a vacation. Vault is Gary’s memory, with the proof attached. The desk still makes the call.

WHAT COULD GO SIDEWAYS

  • Messy old specs2021 records describe stock differently than 2025 records. Discovery measures cleanup cost upfront.
  • Yesterday's pricesStock costs shift. Old matches flag as historical reference, never current pricing.
  • The customer factorContracts and relationships alter final rates. Vault provides the baseline; the desk sets the price.
  • Zero matchesWhen history comes up blank, Vault admits it and routes the request to a human.

What Vault will never do.

  • Send anything to a customer autonomously.
  • Alter system records or pricing tables.
  • Overrule the person on the desk.

Every edge-case failure logs expected vs. actual behavior, root cause, and fix.

1Working assistantA basic assistant the team can try on real questions, with a log of every request.

You'd see. A chat box that answers general print questions. Answers appear as they are written, and an admin can open a log of every request and what came back.

2Answers from our recordsAnswers from our own jobs, with sources, and a score for how often they're right.

You'd see. Answers built from our records, each with numbered sources you can open. When the records don't hold the answer, it says so. A scorecard rates its answers against questions we already know the answers to, and lists which kinds of question it handles reliably.

3Actions with approvalDrafts real work, like a quote or an email, and holds it until a person approves.

You'd see. A draft quote or email showing the jobs it used. A person can edit it, approve it, or reject it.

4MemoryTracks every job: drafted, approved, pending. Work picks up where it left off.

You'd see. A list of every request the assistant handled and where each one stands. After a restart or a crash, the list is still there.

5Reliable multi-step workThe full task from request to approved result, pausing for a person wherever a decision is needed.

You'd see. Each job shows its current step. It can wait days for an approval and then carry on from there. A step that fails is retried or flagged for a person.

6Testing and measuringA scorecard of accuracy, speed, and cost per task, compared with where we started.YOUR CALL: ROLL OUT?

You'd see. A scorecard of test scenarios, with accuracy, speed, and cost per task next to the Discovery baseline. A failed case opens a step-by-step record of what happened. It starts with about 20 questions and grows as real cases turn up.

7RolloutThe assistant in daily use, with secure access, monitoring, and a written recovery plan.

You'd see: The assistant behind a login, starting with a small group. An admin page shows usage, failures, and cost, and an alert goes out when something breaks. Recovery steps are written down, and access follows the permissions we already have.

8ResultsBefore-and-after against the target, with a recommendation.YOUR CALL: WHAT'S NEXT?

You'd see. A one-page report that compares before and after against the Discovery target. It adds feedback from the people who used it, the cost, and the risks, and ends with a recommendation. The code, tests, and records of failed runs go in an appendix.
